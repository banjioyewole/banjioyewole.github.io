new-modality
The mouse and keyboard are dead.

At a minimum, new-modality is a Mac app that gives the globe key on your computer superpowers. Tap it to dictate into any app. Hold it and speak to launch apps, run shortcuts, or navigate menus. A quick double tap is treated as Enter, saving time when working with agents. Add the key beside it and you're talking to Siri, or to an agent that takes the steps on your Mac for you.

At a maximum, the mouse and keyboard are dead, and new-modality mobile killed them. The iOS app is just this globe close to your thumb and a trackpad everywhere else. Grab the globe and drag: each of eight directions is its own action, covering everything the keys do on the Mac, while leaving room for some customization. The flow for working with agents is extremely simple: tap to dictate, pull left to delete, and pull right to full send it with Enter.

The big idea is that dictation no longer has to be perfect. When the thing you're talking to is a model, it can take fuzzy input and use context to work out exactly what you meant. Eighty percent accuracy is enough to communicate a hundred percent of your will. The speech models run locally on Apple silicon, fast enough on a five-year-old M1 Max to type as you speak, and transcription never leaves your Mac. With the on-screen globe and a touchscreen MacBook Pro, you don't even need the globe key. Now that's a whole new modality.

I built it with AI-assisted development, sometimes with multiple agents working on different parts of the project at once, and using new-modality along the way made that faster still.
